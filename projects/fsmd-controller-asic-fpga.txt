title: Design, Synthesis and Comparative Analysis of a Custom FSMD Controller using ASIC and FPGA Technologies
category: ASIC & FPGA
summary: A SystemVerilog FSMD controller explored through Cadence ASIC synthesis and Vivado FPGA implementation.

[details]
I wrote a custom FSMD controller in SystemVerilog with a datapath that multiplies two 4-bit inputs and returns an 8-bit result. I started by checking its behaviour in simulation. In the SimVision waveform both inputs are 15 and the output reaches 225.
I then took the design through an ASIC flow using Cadence Genus. The report lists 117 cells and a cell area of 295.488. Setup timing was met with 6793 ps of slack on a 10 ns clock. Total power was about 10.45 uW with registers accounting for 74% and logic for 22%. I also worked with the Innovus scripts for initialization, placement, clock tree synthesis, routing and signoff.
For the FPGA implementation I used Vivado 2020.2 with an Artix-7 device (xc7a100tcsg324-1). Synthesis completed without errors or warnings. The implemented design used 23 LUTs, 26 flip-flops and 18 IOs. Timing passed after synthesis but failed after implementation: the worst negative slack was -1.592 ns with 8 failing endpoints out of 80. Total on-chip power was 0.092 W of which about 99% was device static power.
Comparing the reports helped me understand how the same RTL design behaves in ASIC and FPGA flows. It also gave me practice reading area, timing and power results without overlooking a limitation such as the FPGA implementation timing failure.


[features]
Custom FSMD controller with a 4-bit x 4-bit multiplier datapath
Functional simulation in Cadence SimVision (15 x 15 = 225)
ASIC synthesis in Cadence Genus: 117 cells, setup slack of 6793 ps met
ASIC power report: about 10.45 uW in total
Place and route flow scripts for Cadence Innovus
FPGA synthesis and implementation in Vivado 2020.2 for Artix-7
FPGA result: 23 LUTs, 26 flip-flops, 0.092 W, timing not met after implementation
Side-by-side comparison of area, timing and power for ASIC and FPGA
